I teach, build and study how AI systems work — and how they can correct mistakes without becoming easier to manipulate.

Over the past few years I have mentored students in AI through Inspire, delivered talks and lectures, and helped small businesses and mayoral candidates understand and integrate AI. I create content, contribute to the AI community and appear as an expert guest on podcasts. I develop open-source repositories with a following, and I have built an audience on X around Claude, coding harnesses and AI safety.

Across that work the interest is the same: making powerful tools understandable, useful and open to scrutiny. Two current projects carry it — research into developmental environments and correction, and a voice interface for directing and hearing that work.

Current projects

The Alignment Hypothesis

Question. Can developmental environments with persistent consequences, relationships and opportunities for legitimate correction help AI systems remain useful, truthful and correctable?

State. A research proposal and initial measurement tools: a partial synthetic environment, scoring tools and a prepared reporting/correction pilot. The model pilot has not run. A history-versus-strong-debrief comparison is outlined for future work.

Go Screenless

Question. Can you direct serious AI work and hear its progress without living at a screen?

State. A phone/Watch prototype and a versioned listening inbox exist. The complete capture-to-agent-to-response loop is still being integrated and tested.

Titus — coordination workflow

Question. Can work and critique across different AI providers be organized, inspected and resumed from shared records?

State. Titus is the coordination workflow I am developing for that. Supporting tools and local conformance tests exist; unattended continuity across hosts has not been demonstrated. Whether multi-model review helps over one strong model is a question I want to measure — the advantage is unproven.

Go Screenless — hear it

Go Screenless is a voice-first interface for working with AI through an Apple Watch or pendant and an iPhone: speak an instruction, keep it attached to the right work, and listen to concise responses with links to the underlying evidence. A prototype phone/Watch app and a listening inbox exist; reliable capture, independent task execution, recovery and device playback still need their own acceptance evidence.
