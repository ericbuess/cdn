The unifying method: capable systems, independent challenge, human judgment.

Why I call it Titus.

Shared purpose, honest judgment and the willingness to address what still needs attention.

I named Titus after the biblical figure Paul trusted to carry difficult work forward and help set things in order. That is the direction I’m trying to give this project: the tools, working context, project records and ways of checking one another’s work.

I want it to carry my corrections forward, help me notice where an idea has drifted and leave a clear account of what was actually done. I use it in my research and software work, while testing its limits and learning from failures. My hope is to spend more time on useful work and be more present with my family.

How to contribute.

I welcome focused criticism, replication and practical advice.

  1. Review the measurement.

    Challenge the scoring, controls and comparison design in the initial alignment evaluation. A useful criticism should help identify what the test can establish and where it could mislead.

  2. Help reproduce the initial cases.

    Check whether the evaluation cases and scoring behave as described, and document failures as carefully as successes. This groundwork comes before the first model pilot.

  3. Share relevant work or practical support.

    Pointers to related research, suitable model access, compute support or potential collaborators would help. Please get in touch if you see a specific way to improve or test the work.
