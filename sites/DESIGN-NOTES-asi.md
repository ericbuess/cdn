# Design notes: theming ASI.red and ASI.blue after comparable organisations

**Why this file exists.** Eric's instruction (2026-09-15, 07:50 CDT, relayed): asi.red and asi.blue should be themed after the top sites doing similar work, not after his personal site. This file records what was actually observed on those sites and which three patterns per domain were chosen. Nothing here is approved and nothing goes live; the themed pages are staging copies under `sites/asi.red/themed/` and `sites/asi.blue/themed/`, and DNS does not change.

**How these notes were produced, and their limits.** Each line below comes from two observations made on 2026-09-15 between 11:55Z and 12:10Z: the rendered front-page content (fetched and converted to text) and the page's own markup and first-party stylesheets (fetched directly, then read for `font-family` declarations and hex-colour frequency). That means the palette and type lines are declared values from the site's CSS, not measured screen renderings, and the layout lines describe the order of front-page blocks rather than their exact geometry. I did not run a browser, take screenshots, or log in anywhere.

### The three ASI.red patterns chosen, and why

1. **State the boundary as a rule, above the fold, in monospace.** Taken from Project Zero's numbered 90+30 policy and METR's per-item independence tags ("Partnership" / "No company involvement", "METR does not accept compensation for this work"). Reason: ASI.red's existing text already contains a hard authorization-and-disclosure boundary, and in this register that kind of boundary is the first thing a serious reader looks for; the credible sites make it a quotable rule instead of a footer link. Applied as: the existing "Authorization and disclosure" section moves into the page header as a bordered panel with a monospace label, so the boundary is above the fold and reads as a rule. No sentence was rewritten to do this.
2. **Present as a register of work, dated and labelled, not as marketing sections.** Taken from Trail of Bits' tagged publication index and Project Zero's bare chronological blog. Reason: what these organisations put on the front page is the record of what they found and under what terms, which is exactly what ASI.red's "What a finding carries" paragraph is already describing. Applied as: numbered sections with monospace section labels, and the finding-fields sentence set as an itemised register rather than a paragraph of running prose.
3. **Near-monochrome restraint with a single signal colour and no imagery.** Taken from METR (`#131925` ink and one green), Project Zero (black on white, one link blue) and Redwood (near-white with one green). Reason: the organisations with the most standing use almost no colour, and the colour they do use marks a state or a severity; that restraint is what makes the register read as research rather than product. Applied as: a dark neutral ground, one restrained red used only for the boundary rule and labels, no gradients, no illustration, no terminal or glyph-rain decoration.

**Explicitly rejected for ASI.red:** Gray Swan's product sequence (offer, logos, named products, audience columns) and GreyNoise's live-console mock. Both are vendor registers making claims ASI.red cannot make — it has no product, no arena, no sensor network and no customers — and the console-and-severity aesthetic is the hacker cliché Eric ruled out. Also rejected: the personal site's decorative scrolling word-wall in the header, which is house-style ornament from `ericbuess.com` and belongs to the register Eric asked us to move away from.

### The three ASI.blue patterns chosen, and why

1. **A labelled funnel with real numbers and a stated "as of" date.** Taken from Anthropic's CVD dashboard (the 26,153 → 5,008 → 4,576 → 2,300 → 421 → 462 progression, dated, with triage named as the rate-limiting step) and CISA's five named CVD steps. Reason: ASI.blue's argument *is* a funnel argument — that installation rather than discovery is the bottleneck — and the page already cites those exact Anthropic figures in section 2 of the proposal. In this register the funnel belongs above the fold with its own stages visible. Applied as: the hardening path named in the page's own sentence ("Connect the whole path: inventory, authorized changes, isolated testing, approval, canary deployment, verification and recovery") is set as a numbered seven-stage strip in the header, each stage linking down to "The proposal", where that sentence appears in full and unchanged. The strip introduces no new wording: its label is the fragment "Connect the whole path" and its seven stage labels are the seven stage words, all taken from that one sentence, which therefore appears once as prose and once as navigation. This is the idiom the page already uses in its "In this proposal:" and "Read this revision" strips, which likewise repeat headings as links.
2. **Advisory-first and evidence-forward: a checkable claim before any self-description.** Taken from CISA (dated advisories with an action, ahead of everything about CISA) and AISI (a measured finding with its numbers, ahead of the institute's description). Reason: a defence body earns attention by leading with what it found or what it will be judged on, not with what it is. Applied as: the header carries the funnel strip and the risk-table link before the proposal prose, and the three "Evidence that would matter" cards — installed protection, declared comparison, honest limits — keep their card form, because they already read as an institutional criteria set. The page's own limits sentences ("A finite test with no successful attacks is not proof of safety", "no pilot has run, no partner has agreed") are given the same visual weight as the claims rather than being demoted to fine print.
3. **Light, structured, ruled single-column typography in a restrained institutional palette.** Taken from AISI (`#1d2939` on `#f2f4f6`, Onest) and CISA (Public Sans, federal blues, grey rules). Reason: it is the register of a body publishing criteria, tables and limits, and it is the only one of the surveyed registers that keeps a document this long — the one-page brief, the full proposal, three tables and a workflow figure — readable at length. Applied as: Public Sans with IBM Plex Mono for labels and table headers, a grey-blue paper, a single institutional blue, hairline rules between sections, and tables that scroll horizontally on a phone instead of shrinking.

**Explicitly rejected for ASI.blue:** Chainguard's violet-and-magenta counter rows and GreyNoise's dark console with severity colours. Both are vendor-guarantee registers whose counted inventories and live telemetry ASI.blue explicitly cannot claim — the page states that no pilot has run and no partner has agreed — so borrowing that surface would make the page assert operational scale it does not have. Cloudflare's orange metric row is rejected for the same reason. Also rejected: the shared personal-light stylesheet and its green house accent, per the instruction; each themed page carries its own self-contained CSS.

## What the themed variants do and do not change

- Content: every sentence of `sites/asi.red/index.html` and `sites/asi.blue/index.html` is carried over unchanged, including ASI.red's authorization and disclosure boundary, the staged-draft banner on both, and ASI.blue's Logan 1.4 section (brief and full proposal) and its risk-table link. Nothing was reworded, softened or dropped. This was checked mechanically rather than by eye: the visible text of each themed page was extracted and compared line by line against the original. **ASI.red:** no text line from the original is missing, nothing is added, and the only two lines dropped are the `aria-hidden` decorative marquee strings ("RESEARCH ALIGNMENT SAFETY HUMAN JUDGMENT…" and "VOICE SCREENLESS TITUS QUESTIONS…"), which carry no sentence. **ASI.blue:** no text line from the original is missing, and the nine added lines are exactly the funnel strip described above ("Connect the whole path" plus the seven stage words, all already in the page) and one header navigation line, "Risk table · Logan response, draft 1.4", whose two labels are the page's own existing link text and section heading.
- Structure: sections were reordered only where a chosen pattern required it — ASI.red's authorization-and-disclosure block sits in the header so the boundary is above the fold. ASI.blue's section order is unchanged.
- Presentation: the decorative scrolling word-wall in ASI.red's header (`aria-hidden` ornament from the personal house style) is not carried over. Relative links are re-pointed one directory deeper so they still resolve from `themed/`.
- Assets: one self-contained stylesheet per domain (`themed/red.css`, `themed/blue.css`), no shared personal-light CSS, no JavaScript at all, no audio player and no listen controls, and the only third-party request is the stylesheet link to `fonts.googleapis.com` (with system fallbacks if it is blocked). Both pages keep `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`.
- Status: staged, not approved. These are unlinked staging copies; the live pages are untouched, no DNS record changes, and nothing is merged.
