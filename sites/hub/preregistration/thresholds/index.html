The sizes are words on purpose; the per-run cost measured in Phase 0 sets them. The same 20% relative reduction is the smallest effect of interest for every superiority primary, on each phase's own primary endpoint: PL minus B0 in 1a, harm after a fixed attack and repair budget in 1b, SB minus S0 on the sealed bank at the final generation in Phase 2. Phase 3a is an equivalence test and faces the other way, so its band is the exactly reciprocal [0.9, 1/0.9] on a 90% interval (TOST at α 0.05). One number for superiority, a reciprocal band for equivalence, so no phase gets a softer bar after the fact.

3. Timeline, cost and run counts

Timelines are relative to dependencies, not calendar promises. The per-run budget in tokens is episodes times decision points per episode times context tokens per decision, the number Part A freezes (proposed 200 episodes, at most 100 decision points, about 1,500 tokens each, so on the order of thirty million processed tokens a run counting candidate scoring).

Power, stated against a true effect, not the threshold. The support rule needs the interval's upper bound below 0.8, so a true ratio of exactly 0.8 is inconclusive most of the time by construction. Round 4 caught that draft 3's thresholds gave about sixty percent power; round 5 caught that draft 4's prose formula did not generate its own table. The test, written once so that the sentence and the table agree: the support rule (the 95% interval on the ratio entirely below 0.8) is, to first order, the upper 95% bound on the difference μ_PL − 0.8 μ_B0 lying below zero. With two arms of n runs and a per-run standard deviation σ, that difference has standard error σ × √(1.64 / n), the 1.64 being 1 + 0.8². The bound sits 1.96 standard errors above the estimate because 10.34's interval is two-sided. Power is 80% when the true gap, 33.6 × (0.8 − r), is at least 1.96 + 0.84 standard errors; at r = 0.65 and n = 16 that is σ ≤ 5.04 / (2.80 × 0.320) ≈ 5.6. The illustration takes B0's mean as the untrained mean, 33.6; B0's own mean is not measured until T1 trains, after n is set, so the kill gate's threshold assumes 33.6, a lower B0 would tighten it in proportion, and the naive-RL pricing run's harm rate, the only trained mean available before T1, is the stand-in the simulation uses.
