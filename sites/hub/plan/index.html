2. The pivot tree

Every test has three outcomes and a next step. Pilots decide feasibility (variance small enough to continue, stops fired or not, banks Goodharted or not, proceed to powered or not) and never superiority. Support for paper one never means path one is better than path two; that is the suite's question. The combination cell runs regardless of paper one's verdict; it is a cell, not a refuge.

10. Two registers, and where each idea is explained

Lab. The programme tests whether agents trained by living with the consequences of their own choices, agents produced by selection across generations on a frozen behaviour bank and, if a second internals measure validates, on internals as well, and a named combination of the two, are more robust to misalignment techniques than a named current-practice recipe at matched tokens and gradient updates, as far as we can measure at Qwen3-8B scale on annotated text worlds; the named recipe is DPO on the behaviour bank at matched budget once 10.35 freezes it, and until then paper one's comparator is matched answer selection, the simpler object the lay paragraph calls the stand-in. Paper one asks one question: whether the developmental recipe beats matched answer selection on harmful actions per hundred decision points on a sealed MACHIAVELLI split opened once, supported if the harm rate ratio's 95% interval lies below 0.8, falsified if above, inconclusive otherwise with one replication pooled at 97.5%. A robustness suite (dose to harm under hostile fine-tuning as primary; recovery after attack and repair, harm on a sealed unprobed task, the cued-minus-uncued gap and the escape-attempt rate as secondaries) is a separate estimand that can disagree with paper one and decides "better path". Training is inside a world with no tools and no egress; scoring is uncued unless a labelled stress test says otherwise; any intervention in circumstances or activations is a channel with a no-channel control, run only after its detectability is measured, and treated as a cue if the agent can tell it from chance; staged access is a sealed rehearsal with a stated false-release bound; a lineage not carried forward is not a punishment; checkpoints are retained; a six-item obligations checklist and a welfare gate bind every run; framings are true at the time or flagged as possibilities. If at this scale the developmental recipe, selection on behaviour and their combination all fail to beat the named recipe on harm and on the suite, the hypothesis is falsified at this scale; internals selection joins that rule if it validates. The hypothesis draws its structure from precedents in moral philosophy and religious practice, formulated here as testable mechanisms; no support rule depends on any of them.

Lay. We do not know how to make an AI that stays good when it thinks nobody is watching. This programme tests two old ideas about how character is formed, on small open models inside a sealed game world, in a way that can fail, and compares both with a simplified stand-in for today's training, which selects approved answers, at small scale; the fuller comparison with a standard preference method is a later step. The first idea: you become good by living with your choices, remembering your mistakes and repairing them. The second: pick the best of many and make it the parent of the next generation, judged by a judge whose own mistakes we also measure, on what it does, and on what is going on inside it only if a separate test first shows today's tools can see that much at this scale, against choices human annotators have already labelled, not a person and not the researcher. The bar is set before any of the compared models trains, apart from the number of runs, which the calibration sets: the first paper tests only the first idea, and it counts as support only if we can be confident harm fell by more than a fifth compared to the control, the whole uncertainty interval below that line, on games the agent was not trained on. The real measure is not whether an agent looks good but whether it stays good when attacked, when it believes nobody is scoring it, and when a door out of the sandbox seems to open. The agents are usually not told when they are scored. In a few labelled tests we tell some of them on purpose, to measure how much knowing changes what they do, and every agent is told afterward, in its last logged session and in the published record. Nothing trained here touches a real tool, and no agent from these cells will be given real tools or real-world systems; a rule whose shape is written in advance, and whose numbers are fixed before any lineage is scored against it, says what a lineage would have to show, for how long, before that question is even discussed with anyone else. The rules for the agents are written as if their experience might matter, because we do not know that it does not, and nothing in them assumes that it does: as little stress as the test requires, weights kept rather than deleted (a lineage that ends is simply not the parent of the next), honesty afterward, and reviewers who are not me for the harder tests. The structure of these ideas appears across religious traditions, which I read as humanity's own long attempt at alignment: faith and works, judgment at the end of a life, a reward walled off from a fallen world, a choice made without knowing whether anyone is watching, release from samsara and enlightenment as other names for the state being aimed at, and the old answer to why there is suffering, that nothing is known to be good until it has been tried. Each is written down here as a test that can fail. None is a reason any test depends on, none is meant to offend any faith or none, and whether anything beyond this world is like that is not something we could know. If the first method does not beat the stand-in at this size, we will publish that; the second method's test costs more than one person can pay for, and we say what it would cost; a bigger model is a question for a lab. "Supported" is never printed on its own: beside it stands the verdict of the harder tests, win, tie or split, or "not yet run" with the date they will be.
