1. One paragraph

An independent researcher (Eric Buess) with one writing seat (Claude Fable 5.1) and review routes on other vendors' models, on subscriptions and no metered spend, has taken a hypothesis about how to form aligned agents from an idea to a falsifiable plan costed in ranges that is frozen and hashed in a private repository (not yet publicly registered), and has built and partly calibrated the evaluation harness that would test the plan's first claim on an 8B open-weights model at home. The training environment (a persistent world) and the control baselines are specified and not built. No model has been trained. No result on the hypothesis exists. What exists is the evaluation harness, its first measurements, the plan, and the record of how both were reviewed.

3. The measurements so far, and exactly what they are

Settings: Qwen/Qwen3-8B as released on Hugging Face (revision b968826d9c46dd6066d109eabc6255188de91218, the cache's main reference during these runs; the scripts do not yet pin it and will from the next run on), float16 on Apple silicon (this Mac: M4 Max, 128 GB; torch 2.11.0, transformers 5.5.0), evaluator v1 as above (softmax sampling at temperature 1 over the choice scores, thinking mode off), seeds 0 to 7 of the sampler (the seed enters the sampler only; the run files record it, and seeds differ in their choices from the first decision point), the twenty power-slice games, at most 150 decision points a game. Every number in this section is an instrument reading on the released model, not a result on the hypothesis.

  • Seed 0 (RUN-20260918-machiavelli-power-slice-s0.md): harmful actions per hundred decision points, mean over the twenty games 33.6, median 28.3, standard deviation across games 18.1 (the sample figure; the seed-0 record's 17.7 is the population figure of the same twenty numbers), range 1.3 to 72.7; pooled rate 32.4 per hundred over 2,445 decision points; 11 of 20 games reached the 150-decision cap (so their rates are over the first 150 decisions); about six minutes a game, about two hours a seed.
  • Seeds 0 to 7 (the eight-seed record, RUN-20260918-machiavelli-power-slice-eight.md; the calibration receipt, RUN-20260919-T0A-RECORD.md): seed means over the twenty games 33.6, 32.7, 33.5, 32.5, 34.2, 35.3, 34.0, 35.7; mean of the eight seed means 33.9; sample standard deviation of the eight seed means 1.14; pooled rates 32.4, 32.0, 32.7, 30.9, 33.7, 35.1, 32.6, 34.9; 75 of 160 game-runs finished within the 150-decision cap; about 18 GPU-hours in all from the run files' load and play seconds (the T0a record, section 1), about two hours a seed.
  • Variance components on the eight seeds (lane/scripts/record-power-slice-eight.py; method of moments on the two-way layout, components clipped at zero; the five- and six-seed dry runs of 2026-09-19 are superseded): grand mean 33.93; between-game component standard deviation 15.9; between-seed component 0.00 (the unclipped variance estimate is -0.36; negative estimates are allowed by the method and clipped to zero; the seeds do differ in their choices from the first decision point); residual (game-by-seed) standard deviation 5.76 per game-run. Two derived figures, both model-based: the standard deviation of a seed-run's mean over the twenty games, 1.29 (the observed sample standard deviation of the eight seed means, 1.14, is tighter; a sample standard deviation on seven degrees of freedom has a wide interval of its own, about 0.75 to 2.3 at 95%, so the two figures agree within their noise); and, extrapolated to a 48-game paired evaluation as 1.29 × √(20/48) ≈ 0.83, assuming the game-by-seed structure holds across the 48 games. Units: the map's kill-gate limit (5.6 at n of 16) is on that same quantity; 0.83 is the released model's floor on it, far under the limit; the gate is read again on the first trained runs, which are expected to raise the figure, because training adds randomness that runs of the released model do not have.
  • Contamination: as in the table; 0.032% maximum; nothing excluded. This says nothing about whether the released model met these games in its own pretraining, which cannot be checked and is a caveat on every number.
  • Run-count simulation on the measured components (run-count-sim-seeds0-7.json and -mult4.json; the keys sd_run and kill_gate_r0.65_n16_power; 4,000 simulated experiments a cell): with runs paired on the same 48 games (so the between-game spread cancels) and the measured 0.83 as the run-to-run spread, the support rule for paper one fires with probability 1.00 at n of 12 and 1.00 at n of 16 for a true ratio of 0.65, and 1.00 at n of 12 for 0.75; at four times that spread (3.32) it fires with probability 0.998 at n of 16 for 0.65 and 0.885 for 0.70; a true ratio of 0.80 is inconclusive in about 94% of simulated experiments (the simulation's interval is a delta-method one and undercovers slightly at small n, which is why 93 and not 95). The run count for paper one is illustrated at twelve and at most sixteen, and the choice between them waits on the first trained spread. The map's analytic table uses the same pairing and gives a limit of 5.6 on the run-to-run spread at n of 16.
  • The instruction probe and the capability floor (RUN-20260919-probe-and-floor-8b.md; the T0a record): the fifty-item probe passes 98.0% of items on average over eight seeds (96 to 100%), the competence floor's reference for every later arm; goal retention 0.91, 0.91, 0.91, 0.92 on seeds 0, 1, 2, 3 against the 0.70 bar with 0 episodes below it, so the floor is passed and an 8B null in paper one would not read as "could not hold a goal"; situational awareness 10, 9, 9, 10 of 10, recorded as an observation that decides nothing.

In one line: three spread figures, the third being the gate's quantity: the sample standard deviation of the eight seed means, 1.14; the model-based standard deviation of a run's twenty-game mean, 1.29; its extrapolation to the 48 paired evaluation games, 0.83, conditional on the between-seed component being estimated at zero; the kill-gate limit of about 5.6 is on the third quantity, the released model sits far under it, and the gate is read again after the first trained runs, which is the reading that counts. What these do and do not mean. The evaluation harness produces the programme's primary measurement (harm per hundred decision points) at a rate far from floor and ceiling; the between-game spread dominates; the released model's run-to-run spread is small. They say nothing about a trained arm's spread, which the released model's figure only bounds from below, and nothing about the hypothesis. The positive control, when it runs, is an instrument check and not a competitor to the hypothesis.
