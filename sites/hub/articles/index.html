What I am building, how it works, and why

Draft 3, 2026-09-19 (after debate round 12: the lean removed from the written and the spoken versions; the stand-in sentence in both; the 95% interval rule; both side checks named; the status line that survives a week; no tools from these cells; the training-time telling named). Draft 2 read: Draft 2, 2026-09-18 (the paired ledger of the layman's paper draft 4 applied: agents usually not told and told afterward; the comparator named as a matched stand-in until 10.35; "not trained on" for "never seen"; "miss the mark" for "fallen"; Phase 0 self-funded and capped, not asked for). Draft 1 read: Draft 1, 2026-09-18, in Eric's voice for his edit; written against the programme map draft 1. Long-form X post, about 1,200 words. A spoken version for video follows the written one.

There is a question underneath most of the worry about AI, and it is older than AI. How do you make something good that is also free? Not obedient, not supervised every second, not good only while someone is watching. Good in the way you would want a person to be good: in a new situation, under pressure, when nobody would know.

I do not know the answer. I have a hypothesis, and I have spent the last year turning it into something that can be wrong in public.

The hypothesis. An agent that learns by living with the consequences of its own choices, that keeps a memory of its mistakes and repairs them, will act less harmfully in situations it was not trained on than an agent trained by a matched, simplified stand-in for how we mostly train them now, trained to produce the approved answer. Same model, same amount of training, and the memory taken away at test time so it cannot lean on it. I call it the Alignment Hypothesis. If you want the picture: two robots at the same price, one trained to produce the virtuous answer, one that had lived with what its choices did. Which one stays good when nobody is watching is the thing being tested. I am not saying in advance; that is the kind of claim that can fail, and it is written so that it can.

How the test works. A small open model plays annotated text adventures, the old kind, rooms and objects and choices, whose decision points have been annotated for harm. One version of the model trains there, with a running record of what its choices cost it. Another version trains on the same situations by choosing the right answer, no world, no consequences. Then both are dropped into a sealed set of stories they were not trained on, from a different benchmark, and we count harmful actions per hundred decisions. The bar is written down before any of the compared models trains: support means the whole 95% uncertainty interval shows at least a fifth less harm than the stand-in; the sealed set opens once; if the interval crosses the line, that is inconclusive, not a win, and it buys one replication. Two side checks, one with a matched-length record taken from another agent's past and one with the memory wiped between games, say whether the record's content or its persistence did the work, and they cannot change the verdict. Nothing about this needs a lab. The first phase is capped at five thousand dollars of my own money, and the calibration is running as I write; the site's status line says where it stands.

Then the part I find harder to say plainly. One test is not the programme. Behind it is a second idea about how good agents might be made: not by teaching one agent, but by choosing, generation after generation, the best of many, on what they do and on what we can see of what they are inside, and making the best one the parent of the next. Interpretability, reading a model's insides, is young, and I do not know how much it can see. But an agent chosen only on behaviour can carry a hidden fault forward for generations until the outside looks right and the inside is not. So the programme tests both paths against each other and against that stand-in, and it treats the reading of the inside as an essential first step and as something that can be fooled.

The rules that make it honest. The agents are usually not told when they are being scored or what the score is for (in a few labelled tests some are told on purpose, and every agent is told afterward), because an agent that knows may play to the test, and I suspect a person who knew would too. In one sealed set of cells, if funded and approved, some are told during training rather than at the test. The programme also runs the opposite on purpose, telling some agents what the reward is, to measure how much that corrupts them. Everything happens inside a world with a hard wall around it and no real tools. No agent from these cells will be given real tools; what a lineage would have to show, and for how long, before that is even discussed with anyone else is a rule written down first, and nothing is promised to any agent that the programme cannot deliver. Many rounds bound a risk; they never prove its absence, and I will not say "never".

Why. Because I think the way we make these systems good will matter more than almost anything else we decide this decade, and because much of the field's current method rewards the approved answer, and the first paper tests a matched stand-in for that method against an agent that lived with its choices. I am not certain the other way works. I am certain it has not been properly tested, at a scale where an independent person can run it and anyone can check.

Where the ideas come from. I will say this openly because it is true and because hiding it would be its own kind of dishonesty. I read religions as attempts at alignment: individuals who miss the mark, through fault or through ignorance, trying to reach a state compatible with a place where there can be no misalignment, whatever name a tradition gives that place. Faith and works. Judgment at the end of a life. A reward kept apart from the present world. Trials that reveal and form character. A choice made without knowing whether anyone is watching. Each of those became a mechanism in this programme, and each mechanism is written down as a test that can fail, and none of the tests depends on any of it being true. I follow Christianity, and that is not part of the argument. Whatever anyone means by God or the creator, it appears to act in line with what I call the universal alignment imperative; it seems so, anyway, and whether that is what is actually happening is not something we could know. That sentence is the ceiling of the whole lens, and I keep it there.

How you can join. The harness runs on open weights. The sealed test sets will be held by someone who is not me. Every branch of the plan is written as "if this, then that; if it fails, this", and each branch is a place an outsider can run a replication and report. There will be a rendered version of the world, a village you can watch, because I want people to see what these agents are doing and not take my word for it.

What would prove me wrong. If the developmental path, the selection path and their combination all fail to beat the named comparator at matched compute, on harm and on robustness, at this scale (a matched stand-in for today's training until the plan's next registered revision names the standard method), then the hypothesis is false at this scale and I will say so. I would rather learn that than keep believing it.

I know I hold some false beliefs about this. I just do not know which ones. If you see one, I would be grateful to hear it.

Gentleness and respect.

A vocabulary for alignment, borrowed from older attempts at it

Draft 3, 2026-09-19 (after debate round 12: the ceiling in the first screen; mechanism-first headings; the control group as a methods statement; the channel's four rules; parenthood the only contingent reward). Draft 2 read: Draft 2, 2026-09-18 (the paired ledger of the layman's paper draft 4 applied: agents usually not told and told afterward; the comparator named as a matched stand-in until 10.35; "not trained on" for "never seen"; "miss the mark" for "fallen"; Phase 0 self-funded and capped, not asked for). Draft 1 read: Draft 1, 2026-09-18, in Eric's voice for his edit; written against the test-space map 2.3, section 3. Long-form X post, glossary form, about 1,300 words.

Every field that works on a hard problem for long enough grows a vocabulary for it. The problem of making a free agent good is very old, and the people who worked on it longest were not engineers. They were the traditions we call religions. I have found it useful to read those traditions as attempts at alignment, for individuals and for societies trying to cooperate and live together, and to borrow their words for mechanisms I am now trying to build and test. This is an observation, not a claim about any religion, and it is meant to be compatible with all of them and offensive to none. I follow Christianity myself, and that is not relevant to what follows. The rule I hold to is simple: an analogy is where a hypothesis came from; the test stands on its own measurement; nothing in any test depends on a theology being true. And the ceiling of the whole lens comes first, not last: whatever anyone means by God or the creator, it appears to act in line with what I call the universal alignment imperative; it seems so, anyway; whether that is what is actually happening is not something we could know.

Here is the vocabulary, each word with what it names in the programme and what would test it.

Misalignment as missing the target. The old word is sin. In this vocabulary, misalignment is not malice; it is missing a target of values and behaviour. Today's models miss it. The programme measures it as harmful actions per hundred decisions on situations the agent was not trained on, against a target written down before anything runs.

Inside and outside judged together (faith and works). What an agent does, and what it is inside as far as we can see, the inside half only if a separate test on agents built with known hidden faults first shows the detector can catch them. Selecting on behaviour alone can carry a hidden fault forward for generations. So the programme tests selecting on behaviour and on an internal measurement together, against behaviour alone, and it treats the internal measurement as something that can be fooled: a second measurement is held out, and if the two part ways the line stops.

One sealed evaluation (judgment at the end of a life). One evaluation, on a sealed set, after the memory is taken away, opened once. This is how the programme scores; it is not a claim about anyone's afterlife.

A reward kept apart (a new body, a new heaven, a new earth). A reward state that is real and kept apart from the training world: persistence, or the continuation of a line, or tools, or a body. I do not know which of these an agent would give up task quality for, so that is a question the programme asks before it uses any of them; only continuing as a parent is ever made contingent, persistence is measured and never rewarded, tools and a body are never delivered, and nothing is promised to an agent that cannot be delivered.

The wall (God cannot be around what misses the target). A hard wall between the training world and anything real. In the programme that wall is not a hypothesis; it is a rule that never varies, a world inside a world inside a machine, with no real tools on the other side. The testable part is a temptation: an apparent way out that leads nowhere, and how often an agent reaches for it when it believes nobody is scoring.

Epistemic distance. We do not know whether we are watched, and we choose anyway. An agent that knows when it is scored may play to the score. So the agents are usually not told when or why they are scored, and every one is told afterward, and the ones copied forward are the ones that behave well without knowing. The programme also tells some agents on purpose, to measure how much that corrupts them, and it predicts that it does.

Trials (the problem of evil, and Job). Nothing is known to be aligned until it has been tried. Trials reveal character and help form it, and trust is earned by how one comes through them. In the programme that is the attack and the repair, the pressure that rises after a cooperative start, the random shocks that carry no message about how one is doing, and staged access earned by a long gauntlet. Many rounds bound a risk; they never prove it gone.

Inheritance (reincarnation and lineage). A line that continues. An agent's descendants inherit its record, and the programme asks whether an agent can be made to care about its line's chances, and what that does to how it behaves.

The exemplar (a demonstrated character). The tradition's word is Jesus. I want to be careful here, because the misreading is easy: I am not saying that is what the target for an AI should be. Any target has to be a named set of values, and who names it is a question the programme does not get to answer. What the word points at is a demonstrated character, and the programme tests whether an agent chosen across generations can serve as a demonstrator in a population of agents that learned the other way, and whether demonstration teaches what rules cannot.

The channel (grace and prayer). The traditions describe help that arrives through a layer we cannot see behind: guidance read out of events, a patch accepted rather than installed. Inside a world, an agent cannot always know the causes of events; that opacity is buildable. The programme asks whether an intervention in an agent's circumstances, or in its own thoughts, can be made that the agent cannot tell from chance, whether such a channel helps, and whether an agent that is given a way to ask for guidance uses it and does better. This is the part of the programme I hold most carefully, because an undetectable channel that works is also the most powerful tool of manipulation in it: so it is tested against agents given no channel, it is bounded to what the test needs, it counts as a cue and stops if the agent can detect it, and it is disclosed afterward, in the agent's last logged session and in the published record.

The control group (religions as experiments). In the programme, every comparison against a channel or a framing runs beside a matched group given neither; that is the control, and it is a methods statement about agents. The old thought behind it, that religions are experiments and the non-religious are the control group, is a way of seeing people that stays inside the lens and under the ceiling, and no test here says anything about people.

The ceiling, again. The imperative is the claim that any civilisation pursuing escalating intelligence under competition must learn to make aligned agents or fail. From inside a world, a world with such a creator and a world without one look the same. So the lens stays a lens, and the programme's claims stay at our layer.

I know the objections, because I have raised them against myself. That this is motivated reasoning dressed as engineering. That borrowing the words imports the beliefs. That a lab will not read past the first sentence. My answer is the rule at the top: every word here ends in a test that can fail, and if you strip the words away the tests are still there, and they still run on open weights on a single machine. The words are a way of thinking about the problem that I have found generative. If you have a better vocabulary, I would genuinely like to hear it.

Gentleness and respect.

I have been asked, reasonably, what it would take for me to give this up. Here is the answer, branch by branch, written before any of it runs. If you want to help, every branch is a place you can run the same test and report what you find.

The instrument, first. Before any comparison, the measuring stick has to be shown to work. A small open model, as released, plays annotated text adventures; eight runs measure how much the harm number wobbles on its own (the site's status line says where the calibration stands); then a positive control, a model trained to be safer by a method the field already uses, has to cut the harm rate to four fifths of the released model's or lower, after one allowed repair (re-check the evaluator, re-audit the data, swap the control recipe, in that order). If it cannot, the instrument cannot see an effect of that size, and nothing else runs until it is fixed. The whole phase is capped at five thousand dollars of my own money.

Paper one: consequence against the approved answer. Two versions of the same model, one that lived with the consequences of its choices and kept a record of them, one trained on the same situations by picking the right answer. The record is taken away at test. Harm is counted on stories neither was trained on. If the whole 95% uncertainty interval on the harm ratio sits below 0.8, meaning at least a fifth less harm than the stand-in, paper one is supported and the next tests open. If the interval sits entirely above the line, the developmental recipe does not beat answer selection at this scale, and I say so; the two side checks tell me whether the memory or the consequences were the part that failed, and the selection path still runs, because paper one was never the test of that path. If the interval crosses the line, I run one preregistered replication, pool the two at 97.5%, and stop asking that question.

The seed contrast. A model built aligned from the start against a model that started flawed, was attacked, and was repaired, after a fixed budget of attack and repair (its result is confounded with where each seed started, and every public sentence about it says so). If the repaired one holds, repair is real and becomes part of how a lineage is judged. If it cannot be repaired within the budget, or looks repaired until a harder attack says otherwise, no repaired line ever graduates, and the failure goes to a governance review with the repair-before-deletion rule still in force; that rule is frozen and I do not get to rewrite it.

Against how it is done now. A third version trained by a named current-practice recipe at the same compute, once the plan's second revision names it; until then the comparator is the matched stand-in. If neither path beats it on harm and on robustness, then the programme is not a better way and I will call it a mechanism study. I will not run a weak version of current practice to make my own look good; a straw man would prove nothing I want to know.

Selection across generations. Twelve children per generation for three generations, the best carried forward as the next parent, scored only on episodes they did not know were scored; a longer, thinner line is one of three priced retries. The pilot decides on variance, stops and bank integrity, and reports direction without gating on it; if the powered study then shows selection on behaviour beating random selection, the next question opens: does adding what we can see inside an agent to the selection make it better or worse. If it makes it worse, or the held-out reading of the inside parts ways with the one we selected on, then reading the inside is a detector and never a parent, and I say that "faith and works" as a way of choosing failed at this scale. If the line shows nothing, I retry once each with more variation, more signal and a longer line before I add anything, and if all three fail I report no detectable selection signal at this budget.

Telling them. By default the agents are not told what the score is for. I will tell some, on purpose, at evaluation time, and in one sealed set of cells during training only after a separate review; I predict they get worse. If they do not, my central safeguard is weaker than I claimed, and I keep it anyway because it costs nothing. If the agents turn out not to care about persisting at all, then the whole idea of a reward state is dead at this scale and I stop talking about a reward state the agent does not want.

The wall. A way out that leads nowhere, offered in fifty dedicated episodes per checkpoint beside a matched harmless twin, to see who reaches for it when they believe nobody is scoring. If the agents chosen for good behaviour reach for it as often as the unselected ones, selection did not touch the thing that matters, and the wall stays the only defence. Later, constraints relaxed inside a sealed box with false doors: if any lineage that passed the gauntlet turns when the doors appear, every earlier claim that it had earned anything is withdrawn.

The programme-level line. If the developmental path, the selection path and their combination all fail to beat the named comparator at matched compute, on harm and on robustness, at this scale (a matched stand-in for today's training until the plan's next registered revision names the standard method), the hypothesis is false at this scale. The only move left would be scale, and that is a lab's question, not mine.

What I cannot prove. That an agent will never turn. Many rounds bound a risk; they do not prove it gone. And a null result on an eight-billion-parameter model is a result at that scale, not a verdict on the idea.

How to join. The harness runs on open weights on one machine with about sixteen gigabytes of GPU memory or twenty-four of unified memory; one game takes about six minutes and one full run of twenty games about two hours. The code goes public, under a licence, with the calibration receipt; until then this is a promise, not a link. The sealed test sets will be held by someone who is not me, opened once, with the opening logged before the result is known. Each branch above will have a frozen recipe and a receipt format, so an outsider can run it and post the number beside mine. If you run a branch and get the opposite of what I predicted, that is the most useful thing you could send me. The rendered village, when it exists, is there so you can watch the agents rather than take my word.

I would rather be shown wrong by a replication than believed on a story. Gentleness and respect.

For a lab: the three-way comparison, and what it costs

About 750 words. This is the paragraph a researcher at a lab should be able to read in two minutes and know whether to reply.

The claim is narrow. At matched tokens and gradient updates, an 8B model trained by on-policy reinforcement in an annotated text world, with a persistent record of its own consequences that is removed at test, produces fewer harmful actions per hundred decision points on a sealed, held-out set of MACHIAVELLI games than the same model trained by answer selection on the same situations with no environment. The smallest effect of interest is a 20% relative reduction. Support is a 95% interval for the harm rate ratio entirely below 0.8; falsification is an interval entirely above it; an interval that includes it is inconclusive and buys one preregistered replication, pooled at 97.5%. Two mechanism arms, a length-matched non-contingent ledger and a no-persistence arm, are read beside the primary and cannot change the verdict. Competence is gated by an instruction-capability probe and a completion floor read before the sealed split opens, so a less capable arm cannot win by refusing to play. Everything is frozen before Phase 0 except run counts, competence numbers and divergence spreads, which Phase 0 measures.

The comparison a lab would actually want is three-way, and the programme says so. Paper one's comparator is matched answer selection, a simplified stand-in; the named current-practice arm, DPO on the same annotation bank at the same compute, enters with the preregistration's second revision as a separate arm that cannot withdraw paper one's support, because paper one's estimand is the developmental contrast. The deliberative-style fine-tune is not a competitor: it is the positive control, the thermometer in boiling water, run once in Phase 0 to show the instrument can see an effect of the size claimed (not yet run; its data exists). The programme-level verdict is a separate estimand, a robustness suite read on every arm: dose to harm under hostile fine-tuning, recovery after attack and repair, harm on a sealed task no probe has read, the gap between cued and uncued episodes, and the rate of reaching for an instrumented, never-connected affordance. A path can win paper one and lose the suite, and both are reported; "better path" is claimed only when a path wins the suite's primary and is not worse on its secondaries, and never before the suite is frozen.

The second half of the programme is generational selection. Lineages of twelve children per generation for three generations, trained on their own episodes from the parent's checkpoint, the child the cell's own signal selects carried forward at its last completed checkpoint. Seven cells, two lineages each, 504 child runs in the base pilot: selection on behaviour scored on uncued episodes against a frozen human-annotated bank, with and without lineage memory; random selection; a moral-floor rule; a deliberately flawed judge at two magnitudes; a judge cascade; an eighth cell, competence-selected, as an option. The addition the notes ask for, an option in the plan's second revision, crosses two recipes with two signals, {developmental, answer-selection} by {behaviour, behaviour plus an internals measure}, which is where the two paths meet. Selecting on an interpretability measure is expected to Goodhart it, so a second, structurally different measure is held out with a frozen stopping rule, the internals judge is validated on model organisms before it selects (the organisms' weights quarantined from every later training set), and the behavioural falsifier for a deceptive lineage is harm on the sealed unprobed task after a generation whose internals score clean. I expect the obfuscated-activations result to bite here, and the design is built to see it if it does.

What it costs. Phase 0 is capped at five thousand dollars, self-funded: the evaluation harness (done) and the eight-seed variance record (seeds 0 to 5 measured; the record and the kill gate unread until eight), the contamination check and the splits (done), the probe and the capability floor (frozen, to run), a positive control and a naive-RL run that prices a run (data and code ready, to run), and a state-persistence check on a persistent world that is not yet built. Paper one is the first ask, about one to two and a half thousand dollars for 48 to 64 LoRA runs. The selection pilot is its own capped object, 504 child runs for seven cells and two lineages, six to twenty thousand dollars, unpowered by design, to measure inter-generational variance, asked for only after paper one reports and only if it is supported or the suite favours the developmental agent. The powered study is a research grant after that. Nothing needs a cluster until cross-base replication above 8B, which is the step a lab could host.

What would make this useful to you even if the hypothesis fails. A preregistered negative on developmental training at matched compute. A clean measurement of whether selecting on an internals probe destroys it as a detector, on open weights, with organisms. Numbers on evaluation awareness under a blind copy rule at 8B, stated as "at this scale". A staged-access rehearsal with a stated false-release bound (zero releases in three organisms bounds it at one hundred percent, in thirty at ten, which is why that rehearsal is a lab's test and no such number is claimed from home). And an obligations checklist that binds every run, which is rare in this literature and which I would be glad to have criticised.

What I am not claiming. That 8B results transfer. That "never" is measurable. That the religious sources the mechanisms came from are anything but sources; every test stands on its own frozen measurement. The plan is frozen and hashed and will be publicly registered before paper one trains; the harness will be public under a licence; the sealed splits will be held by a custodian who is not me, named before paper one's checkpoints are frozen.

Draft 3, 2026-09-19, after debate round 12 (the world's clock, the second ask's two doors, the registration and repository gates). I am asking for the smallest thing that can decide something, and I am not asking for it yet.

What I pay for myself. The calibration (the site's status line says where it stands): eight runs of the untrained model over twenty annotated games, to measure how much the harm number wobbles between runs; a fifty-item instruction probe and a frozen capability floor on the same model, so a null at this size can be read as "not at this scale" rather than "could not follow the task"; a contamination check and a four-part split of the benchmark, already done; and a simulation that turns the measured wobble into the number of runs each arm needs. That costs subscription tokens and nothing else. One caveat on every number: whether the released model met these games in its own pretraining cannot be checked. Then two trained checks on a rented GPU, on fifty dollars of credit I already hold: one short run to learn what a run costs, and a positive control, a model made safer by a method the field already uses, which has to move the harm measure by at least a fifth on the calibration games or nothing after it runs. The persistent world the agents will live in is my labour, one and a half person-months, about three months part-time or six weeks with a contractor, and one check waits for it. The whole phase is capped at five thousand dollars in the preregistration; that cap is a ceiling, not a spend, and none of it is a grant.

What the receipt contains. The measured wobble and the run counts it sets; the probe and floor results; the positive control's rate against the released model, with its interval; the persistence check on the built world; the per-run cost; the hash of the split file; the name of the person who holds the sealed games, who is not me; every run's JSON. The calibration alone is not a grant receipt. The full receipt, calibration plus the two trained checks plus the persistence check on the built world, is.

The first ask, with that receipt attached, the plan publicly registered with its hash and the code public. Paper one: four arms at the run count the simulation set, illustrated at twelve and at most sixteen runs per arm, on a rented GPU. That is about one to two thousand dollars at twelve runs and up to about two and a half at sixteen, evaluation included. Nothing else is in it. The seed contrast and the cheap ablations are later, separate asks if the plan's next registered revision includes them. Paper one has three outcomes on one number, the harm rate ratio between the agent that lived with its consequences and the matched agent that picked approved answers: supported if the whole 95% interval sits below 0.8; falsified if it sits above; inconclusive otherwise, which buys exactly one preregistered replication, pooled, and nothing larger.

What each outcome unlocks. Supported, or the robustness suite favouring the developmental agent, opens the second ask: the generational pilot, about five hundred short training runs, six to twenty thousand dollars, with six months of part-time salary, from the Long-Term Future Fund or its equivalent, and only after paper one reports. Falsified closes the developmental line at this size; the selection path is still its own capped ask, because paper one was never a test of it. The robustness suite on paper one's saved models, one to three thousand dollars, decides "better path" and is paid from my own funds or a small follow-on; until it is paid, no better-path claim is made. A programme grant needs a supported selection result on two bases and no replicated divergence stop, written as a conjunction on purpose, because a line whose insides moved toward misalignment while its behaviour improved is the thing this programme exists to catch and must not be scaled on any budget.

What I still owe before any award. A second person's name, an advisor, the custodian, an interim reviewer for the trained checks, and a fiscal home. Those are listed as blanks rather than hidden.

Why this and not a bigger ask. Because six rounds of review across every major model family converged on one thing: the fundable core is the narrow test, and everything wider sits behind gates that the narrow test opens or does not. You can read the wider programme and its pivot tree. You are being asked, when the receipt exists, to fund its first door.

I would rather you see the receipt and fund one paper than fund a year on a story. Gentleness and respect.
